Most agencies treat an RFI as a bureaucratic formality — something you issue because the process says so, not because you expect to learn anything useful. This guide, developed with funding from the Oregon Department of Transportation Public Transportation Division, makes the case that a well-designed RFI is one of the most cost-effective tools available in a technology procurement.

## What an RFI Buys You

- **Better RFP response rates.** Alerting vendors to a pending procurement early increases the likelihood of getting proposals when the formal RFP goes out.
- **Vendor relationships outside the formal process.** Conversations and demonstrations that start from a written description of your project are far more useful and targeted than cold vendor pitches.
- **Feedback on your assumptions.** Where a project carries known risks or open questions, an RFI invites vendors to react to the directions you're exploring — before you've locked anything in.
- **Peer intelligence.** An RFI can be shared with other agencies that have done similar procurements. What you learn from peers often beats what you learn from vendor responses.

## Dos and Don'ts

The guide's core is a practical dos-and-don'ts list. In brief:

**Do** talk to agencies already using similar systems, give vendors real background on your agency and project, focus on the return on investment you're seeking, and keep the document short.

**Don't** include detailed or final requirements — you probably don't know all the features you want yet, and pre-baking a complete scope can undermine the fairness of the eventual RFP. And don't expect complete candor from vendors: they may still underestimate costs or downplay weaknesses. An RFI is one tool for reducing risk, not a guarantee.

## A Reusable Outline

The guide closes with an outline you can adapt directly: introduction and purpose, agency description, project description framed around results rather than prescribed solutions, key project considerations, questions to put to vendors — including data standards support and data ownership — and response parameters.

## Who It's For

Any transit agency preparing a technology procurement, particularly small and rural agencies without dedicated procurement staff. It's two pages — written to be read, not filed.
