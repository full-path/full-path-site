Most agencies treat an RFI as a bureaucratic formality — something you issue because the process says so, not because you expect to learn anything useful. This guide, developed with funding from the Oregon Department of Transportation Public Transportation Division, makes the case that a well-designed RFI is one of the most cost-effective tools available in a technology procurement.

A good RFI alerts vendors early, builds relationships outside the formal procurement process, and gives your agency a much cleaner picture of the landscape before you lock in requirements for an RFP. It also opens the door to peer learning: sharing your RFI with other agencies that have done similar procurements often yields better intelligence than vendor responses alone.

The guide covers what to include, what to avoid, and provides a reusable outline for structuring your own RFI.
