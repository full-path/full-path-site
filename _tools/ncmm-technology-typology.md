The National Center for Mobility Management developed this typology for mobility managers who find themselves responsible for technology decisions without much background in transit technology — people sometimes called "accidental techies." It gives them a common language and a structured way to compare the many types of technology available to public transportation providers, without requiring deep technical expertise to use. Rather than evaluating specific products or vendors, it categorizes technology by type and rates each category across six dimensions that reflect the full lifecycle of a technology decision.

The typology was published in 2020 and has not been formally updated since. The transit technology landscape has shifted in the intervening years — some categories have matured, new entrants have emerged, and a few of the specific ratings would land differently today. That said, the underlying structure holds up well. The six evaluation dimensions remain the right questions to ask, the category taxonomy still reflects how the field actually organizes itself, and the relative positioning of most technology types is as accurate now as it was then. It's a useful starting point, not a definitive current-state picture.

The typology's categories have had a second life as an organizing device. Full Path's [transit stack diagram]({{ '/tools/transit-stack-diagram/' | relative_url }}) uses them to group the systems an agency actually runs, which is a different job than the typology was built for — inventorying one agency rather than surveying the field — but the category structure transfers cleanly, and it means an agency that has drawn its stack can look up any box on the page in the typology matrix.

The related resources below include the NCMM's published framework and matrix, and an interactive version of the matrix built on Observable that allows for filtering and exploration across dimensions. The interactive version was developed as a working prototype for a dynamic, updateable typology — one that could be maintained collaboratively rather than revised and republished periodically as a static document.
