Full Path works with community transit agencies and the organizations that support them — state DOTs, technical assistance centers, grant-funded programs — on technology strategy, assessment, and implementation. The focus is on getting to the right answer, not the most impressive one.

## Technology Strategy and Assessment

Most technology problems in community transportation are organizational problems in disguise. Before an agency can choose the right tool, it needs to know what it's actually trying to solve, whether it has the capacity to implement and sustain something new, and what constraints — budget, staffing, broadband, vendor support — will shape what's actually viable.

Services in this area include:

- Technology needs assessment and gap analysis
- Vendor and product landscape research
- RFP development and procurement support
- Technology readiness evaluation (see the [Readiness and Resilience Assessment Tool](/tools/readiness-assessment/))
- Strategic planning for technology modernization

## Implementation Support

Selecting a system is the beginning, not the end. Implementation is where most technology initiatives succeed or fail, and it's where agencies with small staffs and no dedicated IT support are most vulnerable.

Services in this area include:

- Implementation planning and project management
- Staff training and change management support
- Data migration and system configuration
- Multi-agency coordination and integration planning
- Business continuity and maintenance planning

## Open-Source Tools

Full Path develops and supports open-source tools designed specifically for small and rural transit providers — organizations that don't have the budget for enterprise software or the staff to maintain it.

### RideSheet

[RideSheet](/tools/ridesheet/) is a free, open-source scheduling, dispatching, and trip coordination system built on Google Sheets and Google Apps Script. It handles the full data lifecycle from scheduling through completed-trip reporting, supports inter-agency coordination via a standardized API, and is designed to be maintained by the organizations using it — no software license, no dedicated IT staff required.

As of early 2026, RideSheet is in active use at seven agencies in four states.
