Starting in 2019, the Oregon Department of Transportation contracted with Full Path, working alongside Trillium Solutions, to assess the technology in use at small and rural transit agencies across the state. As Oregon increased its investment in small and rural transit service, agencies faced a widening array of technology options — scheduling and dispatch software, in-vehicle hardware, rider-facing tools, data systems — with little agency-level capacity to evaluate them. The program's purpose was to give individual agencies a clear, agency-specific look at their technology and its fit with their operations, while giving ODOT a growing, statewide picture of what small and rural agencies actually needed.

Over the following several years, Full Path completed assessments for more than twenty transit agencies and tribal and regional providers across Oregon, each grounded in interviews and on-site visits with agency staff — from leadership down to drivers, schedulers, and dispatchers. The engagement grew to include other formats built on that same foundation: targeted recommendations for agencies with a narrower, specific question in mind; visual [transit stack diagrams]({{ '/tools/transit-stack-diagram/' | relative_url }}) inventorying the systems an agency relied on across categories like payment, in-vehicle equipment, operations, and reporting; and case profiles examining what made a small number of standout agencies particularly successful in adopting technology. The stack diagram in particular came out of this work as a reusable tool — drawing one early in a site visit proved to be the fastest way to get everyone in the room looking at the same picture of the agency's technology, and the template refined across these assessments is now published for any agency to copy.

Beyond the individual engagements, the program periodically stepped back to synthesize findings across agencies for ODOT — surfacing shared patterns and structural gaps that no single agency assessment could reveal on its own. Taken together, the work built one of the more detailed pictures available anywhere of how small and rural transit agencies actually use technology, and gave ODOT an evidence base for shaping its own strategy for supporting that use going forward.
